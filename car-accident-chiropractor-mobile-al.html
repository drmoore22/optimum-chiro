After the Collision

What to Do After a Car Accident

The hours and days after a crash matter for both your health and your records. Here is a practical order of steps.

1. Rule out an emergency

Go to an emergency room first for severe neck pain, a severe headache, weakness, numbness or tingling, confusion, vomiting, or loss of consciousness. Dr. Moore coordinates with your physician or a specialist when a finding calls for it.

2. Get evaluated early, even if you feel fine

Whiplash symptoms are often delayed by 24 to 72 hours or more. An early evaluation gives the clearest clinical picture and records your findings close to the time of injury. Read more about why whiplash symptoms are often delayed and why to be seen even if you feel fine.

3. Keep records

Report the accident to your insurer, keep copies of reports and bills, and tell every provider about all of your symptoms, including headaches, dizziness, and trouble concentrating.

How Dr. Moore Evaluates Auto Injuries

Measuring What Standard Imaging Can Miss

A car accident can injure the ligaments that hold the neck and back vertebrae together. The resulting laxity often cannot be seen on a standard X-ray, and MRI shows the spine in one static position, so it is not a reliable indicator of ligament laxity.

Dr. Moore starts with a detailed history and neuromusculoskeletal examination. When indicated, he takes flexion and extension X-rays and digitizes them in Symverta, which measures translation and angular deviation at each spinal segment. His Fellowship in Primary Spine Care covered the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of spinal ligament injury in depth. Digital Motion X-Ray is available for selected cases.

Common Questions

Car Accident Questions, Answered

What should I do after a car accident in Mobile?

If you have severe neck pain, a severe headache, weakness, numbness or tingling in the arms or legs, confusion, vomiting, or loss of consciousness, go to an emergency room first. Otherwise, get evaluated within the first few days even if you feel fine, because whiplash symptoms are often delayed. Report the accident to your insurer, keep records of every visit, and tell each provider about all of your symptoms.

Do you accept MedPay and auto insurance?

Yes. Dr. Moore accepts MedPay (medical payments coverage) and auto insurance. MedPay can pay for care regardless of who was at fault, up to the limit on your policy. Call or text the office and we can walk through how your coverage applies.

Do you work with letters of protection?

Yes. A letter of protection is an agreement, usually between you, your attorney, and the practice, that treatment costs will be paid from a settlement or other recovery. The terms are set in writing before care begins, and we are glad to explain them. Dr. Moore's findings are documented objectively whether or not a claim is involved.

Do I need an attorney before I can be seen?

No. You can be evaluated without an attorney. If you are working with one, Dr. Moore coordinates records and reports with your attorney's office.

What is in the report personal injury patients receive?

Personal injury patients receive a formal narrative report after care. It documents the history, examination findings, imaging, colorized MRI images, Symverta measurements, treatment provided, and your response to care, so your attorney and insurer have a clear and objective record. The report documents findings and does not promise any outcome in a claim.

How soon after a crash should I see a chiropractor?

Ideally within the first few days, even if you feel fine. Pain can be masked at the moment of impact and inflammation builds over the first 24 to 72 hours. An early evaluation also records your findings close to the date of the accident.
